## 1 · The moment — ethos altitude

### What the calendar says versus what the body says

- **A traditional calendar declares autumn today.** 立秋 Risshū falls 2026-08-07, at the hottest
  point of the year, because it tracks the turn rather than the temperature `SIG-025`
- The microseasons that follow it are named for what is observed, not what is dated: *cool winds
  blow* (8–12 Aug), *evening cicadas sing* (13–17), *thick fog descends* (18–22), then *manageable
  heat* from about the 23rd `SIG-025`
- Japanese carries a dedicated word — 残暑, *zansho* — for heat that persists **after** autumn has
  formally begun `SIG-025`
- Several unrelated European languages name the same late-warm spell: German *Altweibersommer*,
  after the gossamer spider threads that float in early autumn; Slavic *babje leto*; Swedish
  *Brittsommar*, after a saint's day `SIG-026`
- Two of those three personify it as an old woman. The third does not — the pattern is a shared
  image, not a universal one `SIG-026`

**Why this belongs in an evidence document:** a gap between formal season and felt weather that
multiple cultures bothered to name is a durable human observation rather than a merchandising
conceit `SIG-025`. It is not evidence of US demand and must not be read as any.

### The sensory register

**Unsourced.** The following is descriptive register carried for later creative use. It cites no
signal because it is not evidence, and it may not be used to support any claim.

- **Light.** The sun drops, so everything becomes side-lit; the haze of suspended dust and heat
  turns distance soft and blue; the sky hardens as humidity falls
- **Colour.** Not the October postcard. Bleached denim and silvered wood; dry gold — hay, stubble,
  dead lawn; a chalk-grey film on everything; green gone tired rather than turned; the fruit
  colours the only saturated things left
- **Sound.** Cicadas to crickets — heat-of-day giving way to evening. The Japanese calendar marks
  the evening cicada in mid-August `SIG-025`. A marching band on a Tuesday. A screen door
- **Smell.** Cut grass turning to hay; tomato vine; chlorine fading off skin; hot dust before
  rain; the first woodsmoke; the mineral smell of the first cold morning
- **Taste.** The last tomatoes, which are the best ones; stone fruit at the edge; the first hard
  apple; and preserving — canning, pickling, jam, the whole late-summer instinct being *keeping*

### What people are doing

- Asking permission. *When can you start wearing white again · white after labor day · suede boots
  in summer · when can you start wearing linen* `SIG-008`
- Asking how to deploy things they already own, though this signal is weaker than it looks — the
  head term is contaminated by graduation dress `SIG-009`
- Tidying and resetting: *decluttering* ranks sixth under *end of summer* `SIG-011`
- Collecting rather than buying — the largest figure in the year's biggest trend report is a
  **collection** verb, not a purchase one `SIG-002`

### Where the register already lives

**Unsourced, carried for creative reference:** Malick's *Days of Heaven* for the golden field at
magic hour; Wyeth's *Christina's World*; Millet's *The Gleaners*; Coppola's *The Virgin Suicides*;
Keats's *To Autumn*, written after a September walk; Bradbury's *Dandelion Wine*, which is
literally the preserving instinct as a novel. And 物の哀れ, *mono no aware* — the ache of things
passing being the thing that makes them beautiful.

### What this moment is NOT

- **Not October.** No pumpkins, no orange, no cosy — that is six weeks out and it is currently hot
- **Not back-to-school.** The obvious commercial reflex for the date, aimed at children
- **Not a discount moment**, though Labor Day is a major discount weekend `SIG-027`
- **Not a countdown to a better season**

> The three framings above are **excluded by the frame** and are described here accurately as
> research, which the frame explicitly permits. They may not be adopted in strategy or in any
> campaign-facing expression.

## 2 · The evidence — territory altitude

### Demand and behaviour — 10 signals

The strongest cluster, and the one that most constrains naming.

- **The obvious phrase is taken.** *What to wear in September* is a travel-packing query: all ten
  completions are destinations — Italy, Paris, Ireland, Japan, Portugal, London, New York,
  Switzerland, Greece `SIG-006`
- **The workable phrase is *transitional*.** It names *summer to fall* at position three, and
  *meaning* at position four, so people are meeting the term and looking it up. Its year-tagged
  variants are still on **2025** `SIG-007`
- **Product search is transactional, not seasonal.** Fit, colour, audience, *near me*. Almost no
  year tags, almost no *fall* — **except outerwear**, which does carry the season `SIG-010`
- **Seasonal language sits above the product layer, not inside it** `SIG-010`
- **Permission is a live search intent** `SIG-008`
- **Saving carries declared intent.** Users read a cart as *I might want that* and a wishlist as
  *I definitely want that*, and avoid wishlists for perceived setup cost and gift-sharing
  connotation `SIG-014`. **That research is from 2018 and describes retail carts, not a discovery
  platform** — generalising it to this product is an assumption, not a finding
- **The most on-objective source in the scan is unread.** A 2026 peer-reviewed study on
  wishlist-versus-cart placement and purchase likelihood exists and is identified; both the
  publisher page and the ProQuest record were unreadable `SIG-015`. **No finding is claimed from
  it.** Its title implies a direction and that implication is not usable

## 3 · The crossing

**Where the two altitudes agree.** The register's central behaviours — permission, keeping,
resetting — appear independently in the search data: permission queries `SIG-008`, *decluttering*
under *end of summer* `SIG-011`, and a collection verb carrying the largest figure in the year's
biggest forecast `SIG-002`. Three different families point at the same posture, which is the
strongest structural finding in this scan.

**Where they disagree, and it matters.** The moment's best language is search-dead `SIG-013`,
its most obvious phrase belongs to travel `SIG-006`, and its editorial colour has no fashion
search behaviour at all `SIG-012`. Meanwhile the phrase that does carry intent — *transitional* —
is plain to the point of dull `SIG-007`.

**This is not a contradiction; it is a layer distinction.** Evocative language and findable
language do different jobs. Any attempt to make one string do both is what the contamination
signals are warning about.

**Where the register overreaches.** The cross-cultural naming `SIG-025` `SIG-026` is genuinely
interesting and says **nothing about US demand**. It is register, not evidence, and it is
recorded that way. Two of three languages personify the moment as an old woman; the third does
not — a shared image, not a law.

**The honest gap.** The objective is saves. The only save-behaviour evidence in the scan is
eight years old, about retail carts, and explicitly labelled as not generalisable `SIG-014`; the
current study is unread `SIG-015`; and there is no first-party data at all. **Nothing here
establishes what makes this platform's users save.** Any opportunity claiming to know that will
be reasoning from capability, not evidence.

### Unifying ideas — sketches only, not opportunities

Recorded so they are not lost, and deliberately underdeveloped.

- **Keeping.** The season's instinct is preserving `SIG-002` `SIG-011`, and the product's core
  gesture is saving. That is the closest thing to a structural fit in this scan — and it is
  currently an argument from capability, because the save evidence is stale `SIG-014` or unread
  `SIG-015`
- **Permission.** People ask whether they are allowed to wear things yet `SIG-008`, with a dated
  hinge inside the window `SIG-027`. A platform that owns no inventory can say *not yet*
- **The gap between formal and felt.** Named by a calendar `SIG-025`, by several languages
  `SIG-026`, and visible in editorial copy `SIG-004`
- **Restraint as taste.** Curation over acquisition `SIG-001` `SIG-021`, tempered by the fact
  that the restraint position is loud and small `SIG-023`

## 4 · Open questions

**Blocking for opportunity scoring**

- **No first-party behaviour.** Nothing establishes what makes *these* users save. Every
  opportunity will be scored on capability rather than evidence, and L3-by-evidence is
  unclaimable this run
- **`SIG-015` is unread.** The one current, peer-reviewed, directly-on-objective source. A library
  or institutional copy would materially change the evidence base

**Would change the reading**

- Is the four-to-six-week horizon meant to close **before** New York Fashion Week or **across**
  it? `SIG-024` — the answer changes which temporal catalysts are usable
- Does *transitional* `SIG-007` have real volume, or is it a term people look up and abandon?
  Unanswerable without a paid tool
- Is blokecore decaying now the tournament is over `SIG-020`?
- Does the maximalism home-versus-fashion split `SIG-018` survive corroboration?

**Assumed, not established**

- That 2018 retail-cart research `SIG-014` says anything about a 2026 discovery platform
- That late August is still hot in the US market. Treated throughout as common knowledge; **no
  temperature data was pulled**
- That editorial sell-out language `SIG-017` reflects real demand
