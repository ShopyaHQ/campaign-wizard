# Naming, SEO, and the campaign quality bar

Read at Stage 1 before the recon, and again at Stage 3 before naming anything.

> ## Prerequisite — nothing in this file runs first
>
> **`plain_statement.md` comes before every word of this document.** The Plain Statement is the
> first deliverable of every run: the concept in flat bullets, no name, no tagline. Naming does
> not begin until the human has reacted to it and the thinking is agreed.
>
> This file governs what happens **after** that: how the name is pulled out of the agreed
> statement's own prose, and how the keyword strategy lands on collections and rails. If no Plain
> Statement exists yet, stop reading and go write one.

---

## 1 · A campaign is a point of view, not a filter label

**Never build a campaign on a search query.** A keyword finding is *evidence that a tension is
real*. It is not the idea. No brand ships "Wear It Sooner" — that is a filter pretending to be
a campaign.

How real fashion and e-commerce campaigns are actually built (Jigsaw *Love Immigration*,
Lacoste *Timeless*, Hermes *La Maison des Carres*, Nasty Gal *#GirlBoss*): the central idea is
**cultural, emotional or values-led**; the naming is **editorial and thematic, never
descriptive**; the product is **downstream of the point of view**. They build a world, not a
category.

### The quality bar — a concept fails if it cannot pass all four

1. **Fully formed.** A real tagline or thought, in finished English. Not a fragment, not a
   label, not two nouns stuck together. If it would not survive being set in 48pt on a homepage,
   it is not done.
2. **Names a tension, not a category.** "Suede boots and dark denim" is a merchandising plan.
   "The calendar turned. The weather didn't." is a campaign.
3. **Structurally ours.** Ask: *could this run at another retailer unchanged?* If yes, it is
   generic. The best concepts argue for the behaviour this specific product enables.
4. **No AI slop.** No stacked abstract nouns, no "elevated / curated / effortless / discover
   your", no vague uplift. Concrete objects, real situations, actual human phrasing.

### Vibe and SEO are not in tension — they sit at different layers

The campaign carries the feeling. The collections carry the keywords. The rails carry the hook.
Trying to make one string do all three jobs is what produces slop.

## 2 · The three-layer naming model

| Layer | Job | Ranks? | Naming |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Campaign** | the movement, the feeling, the POV | **No — never a search target** | Editorial. Never in a URL, never in a user-facing axis |
| **Collection** | the rankable page — one page, one subject | **Yes — this is the SEO asset** | Keyword-bearing, written with voice |
| **Rail** | the hook on `/explore` | **No — it is a heading, and its job is the click** | Voice-forward, keyword-adjacent |

### Why the SEO weight sits on the collection, not the rail

A rail `name` is a heading on `/explore`. That page carries twenty headings about twenty
different things — it is thematically diffuse and will not rank for any of them. The
**collection page** is the rankable unit: one page, one subject, its own H1.

So the rail's job is **CTR** — earning the internal click from `/explore` — and the
collection's job is **ranking**. Voice on the rail, keywords on the collection.

> **BLOCKING VERIFY:** this holds only if collection pages exist as public, indexable URLs with
> their own title tags. Today view-all resolves to `/feeds/{feedId}`, and whether that is
> indexable is unconfirmed. **If there is no rankable collection page, there is no organic
> surface and the keyword work is decorative.** Confirm before promising SEO value.

### The formula: keyword in the noun, voice in the frame

- WRONG: *"Boots First"* — voice, no keyword. Invisible to search.
- WRONG: *"Suede Boots Women Knee High Ankle"* — keyword, no voice. Reads like a filter.
- RIGHT: *"The Suede Boot, Ankle to Knee"* — the searched nouns are present; the construction
  carries voice.

The modifiers people actually type are free keyword material sitting in natural English. You do
not have to choose between sounding human and being findable.

## 3 · The recon method — what is real data and what is not

**Available and real:** Google's public autocomplete endpoint —
`https://suggestqueries.google.com/complete/search?client=firefox&q=<term>` — fetches cleanly
and returns **ranked query completions**. This is genuine ordering of what people type. Run it
across every candidate territory and note the modifier patterns.

**NOT available — never fabricate these:** search volume, keyword difficulty, and rising-vs-flat
trend direction. Google Trends is JS-gated and strips to nothing. If a number would need a paid
tool, say "unavailable" rather than estimating.

**Also worth pulling:** editorial coverage via search (shows saturation, and where the gaps
are), and retailer category naming (a proxy for what converts).

**Always ask first, before assuming external data is all there is:** does the client have
first-party search data — site search logs, Google Search Console? What users type into *their*
box beats any external proxy.

### Findings that hold until re-tested (2026-08)

- **Modifiers are transactional, not seasonal.** Across boots, denim, jackets, bags the
  completions are fit (*ankle, knee high, wide calf, straight leg, baggy, low rise, high
  waisted, oversized, cropped*), colour (*black, brown*), audience, and *near me*. Almost no
  "2026", almost no "fall". People search the object and its attributes.
  **Exception:** outerwear does carry the season — *fall jackets 2026*, *fall jacket trends 2026*.
- **Colour terms can be contaminated.** *chocolate brown* is dominated by hair colour and
  brownie recipes; fashion appears at position 7. Usable only qualified onto an object
  (*chocolate brown boots*). **Always check a colour term before naming a collection after it.**
- **Question queries belong to the editorial seam, not product rails.** *when can you start
  wearing white again* / *white after labor day* / *suede boots in summer* / *when can you start
  wearing linen*. People ask permission to wear things. That is guide content (seam 5), and it
  is largely unserved.
- **Weak territories:** *what to wear early fall* (polluted by wedding and Niagara Falls
  queries); *fall outfits 2026* (thin — the real anchor is *fall fashion 2026 trends*).

## 4 · The concept stage is inventory-blind

Stage 1 and Stage 3 concepting happen **without reference to what is already in the library.**
The concept is right or wrong on its own merits. Whether existing products happen to fit is a
Stage 4 question, raised there, once — never as a constraint on what gets proposed.

Illustrative example products at Stage 3 exist to show a collection is *real*, not to limit what
can be conceived.
