## 1 · The moment — campaign altitude

### The thing underneath all of it

- **Two feelings at once, and they do not cancel: mourning and appetite**
- Summer ending is the only ending people look forward to. That contradiction *is* the moment
- Everything below is detail hanging off it

### What the calendar says versus what the body says

- `[fact]` **立秋 Risshū — "the beginning of autumn" — began August 7.** A thousand-year-old
  calendar declares autumn at the hottest point of the year, because it tracks **the turn**, not
  the temperature
- `[fact]` The microseasons that follow, in order: 涼風至 **"cool winds blow"** (Aug 8–12) ·
  寒蝉鳴 **"evening cicadas sing"** (Aug 13–17) · 蒙霧升降 **"thick fog descends"** (Aug 18–22).
  Then 処暑 Shosho, **"manageable heat"**
- `[fact]` 残暑 **zansho** — Japanese has a dedicated word for the heat that persists *after*
  autumn has officially begun. Late-summer letters are still addressed with it

### What other cultures NAME this moment

The richest vein in the whole survey. A culture with a word for something has already done the
emotional analysis.

- `[fact]` **Altweibersommer** — German, "old women's summer." The name derives from
  *Altweiberfäden*: the gossamer spider threads that float in the air on exactly these days and
  catch the light like white hair
- `[fact]` **Babje leto** — Slavic, "grandmother's summer." Same personification, arrived at
  independently
- `[fact]` **Brittsommar** — Swedish, after St. Bridget. **Indian summer** — English
- `[inference]` Multiple unrelated cultures looked at this fortnight and reached for **a woman
  past her youth having a brief, undeniable, beautiful spell.** Fashion has not picked this up
- `[fact]` **物の哀れ mono no aware** — the Japanese aesthetic principle: the gentle ache of
  things passing, and the idea that *the awareness itself is what makes them beautiful.* It is
  the precise emotion of late August and the one word for it

### Light

- `[fact]` The sun drops in the sky, so everything becomes **side-lit**. Golden hour genuinely
  gets longer and lower — solar geometry, not a mood
- Shadows at 6pm where there were none in July
- **The haze** — dust, pollen and heat suspended, so anything beyond a mile goes soft and blue
- Honey → amber → then the first evening that arrives early, which is a cold blue
- The sky hardens and deepens as humidity drops. July blue is milky; September blue is not

### Colour — and this is NOT the postcard palette

Orange leaves are October, six weeks out, and everyone jumps straight there.

- **Bleached** — everything the sun has been on all summer. Faded denim, silvered wood, white
  gone to cream
- **Dry gold** — hay, wheat stubble, dead lawn, straw. The gold that is actually a dead plant
- **Dust and chalk** — a pale grey-beige film over everything
- **Green going over** — not turning yet, but tired. Olive, grey-green, dusty sage
- **Sunflowers bowing**, heavy, going brown at the face
- **The fruit colours are the only saturated things left** — tomato red, plum, blackberry, fig
- The palette is **worn**. It is the colour of things that have been used all summer

### Sound

- **The cicada-to-cricket crossfade.** Cicadas are heat and daytime; crickets and katydids are
  evening, and they take over across August. **You can hear the season turn**
- `[fact]` The Japanese calendar marks it — *higurashi*, the evening cicada, the specifically
  melancholy one in their poetry, gets its own microseason mid-month
- The morning a cicada does not start
- A marching band practising on a Tuesday evening. A whistle across a field. Friday night lights
- Distant lawnmower, the last few. A screen door. The county fair a mile off
- Rain hitting hot pavement

### Smell

- **Cut grass turning to hay** — cut and left, drying. Different from June's cut grass
- **Tomato vine.** Sharp, green, one of the most specific smells there is
- Chlorine on skin, fading. Sunscreen going stale in the bottle
- **Petrichor** — hot dust, then first rain
- **Woodsmoke.** One neighbour, one evening, and the season is over
- The first cold morning has a smell — mineral, sharp, like the inside of a fridge
- Windfall fruit fermenting under a tree, and the wasps
- **New notebook, pencil shavings, the vinyl of a new bag.** The September smell

### Taste

- The last tomatoes, which are the best tomatoes, and which collapse
- Stone fruit at the exact edge — peaches that have to be eaten today
- Corn. Figs. The first apple: sour, hard, thrilling
- `[fact]` **Blackberries have a cultural expiry date** — English folklore says that after
  Michaelmas, Sept 29, they belong to the devil and you stop picking
- **Preserving** — canning, pickling, jam. The whole late-summer domestic instinct is *keeping*
- The drink goes from cold to warm without anyone deciding

### What people actually do

- **Last-ness.** Last swim, last barbecue, last white. People count remaining weekends out loud
- **The squeeze.** One more trip, one more night outside
- **Preserving.** Canning, the photo dump, the summer archive. The instinct to *keep* the thing
- **The reset.** September is the real new year for anyone who ever went to school. The urge to
  become a sharper person peaks in the first week of September and is dead by October
- **The crossfade.** Summer clothes worn with autumn intentions

### Where this register already lives

- **Days of Heaven** (Malick, 1978) — the definitive golden-field film, shot almost entirely at
  magic hour. Harvest, wind through wheat, everything ending. The single strongest visual reference
- **Christina's World** (Wyeth) · **Van Gogh's wheatfields** · **The Gleaners** (Millet) —
  the dry field, the gold under a heavy sky, the picking-up of what is left
- **The Virgin Suicides** (Coppola) — end-of-summer suburbia, hazy and elegiac
- **Call Me By Your Name** — a summer with a known end date; the whole film is dread of September
- **Keats, "To Autumn"** — written after a September walk, 1819. The canonical text
- **Fitzgerald** — Nick Carraway: "Life starts all over again when it gets crisp in the fall"
- **Bradbury, *Dandelion Wine*** — summer bottled and stored in a cellar. The preserving instinct
  as a novel

### What this moment is NOT

- **Not October.** No pumpkins, no orange, no cosy. It is not cosy, it is hot
- **Not back-to-school.** The commercial reflex, aimed at children, the most crowded lane in the
  calendar
- **Not harvest-festival kitsch**
- **Not a countdown to a better season.** Nothing here is waiting

## 2 · The evidence — collection altitude

### Autocomplete (ranked query completions, pulled 2026-08-06/07)

**What the season itself returns:**

- `[autocomplete]` **end of summer** → *end of summer **2026*** ranks **above the bare term**,
  then nails · sales · date · **decluttering** · party. One of the very few places a year tag
  appears at all, and *decluttering* at #6 is the reset instinct showing up in search
- `[autocomplete]` **late summer outfits** → women · men · **2025** · women 2025 · **women over
  50** · then bleeds into *end of summer outfits*. Deep and real intent — but the year-tagged
  completions are still **2025**, meaning the 2026 variants have not accumulated yet
- `[autocomplete]` **golden hour** → lyrics · hemp · meaning · tulips · Kacey Musgraves · coffee.
  **Search-dead for us.** Owned by a song
- `[autocomplete]` **indian summer** → movie · meaning · TV cast · rudbeckia · golf course ·
  apple juice. **Search-dead for us**

**What the products return:**

- `[autocomplete]` Modifiers are **transactional, not seasonal** — fit (*ankle, wide calf,
  straight leg, low rise, baggy, oversized, cropped*), colour, audience, *near me*. Almost no
  "2026", almost no "fall". **Exception: outerwear carries the season** (*fall jackets 2026*)
- `[autocomplete]` **Permission queries, and they are unserved** — *when can you start wearing
  white again* · *white after labor day* · *suede boots in summer* · *when can you start wearing
  linen*
- `[autocomplete]` **"how to wear"** → bandana · scarf · cufflinks · bolo tie · pocket watch.
  **Every one an object the searcher already owns**
- `[autocomplete]` **poetcore** → ten completions deep, all fashion-intent, zero contamination
  (*fashion, style, men, aesthetic, outfits, meaning, crochet, trend, clothing*)
- `[autocomplete]` **blokecore** → fashion · meaning · aesthetic · jersey · style · outfits ·
  **men, women**. A gendered split means the term escaped its origin subculture
- `[autocomplete]` **fair isle** → *fair isle sweater* outranks the bare term; then two distinct
  audiences on identical words — *sweater women, cardigan* (fashion) and *knitting, pattern,
  scotland* (craft)
- `[autocomplete]` **maximalist** → decor · interior design · wallpaper · rugs **above** outfit
  and fashion. Maximalism is currently a HOME search behaviour, not a fashion one

## 3 · The crossing — where the register and the data meet

This is the part that matters, and it validates the three-layer model rather than contradicting it.

- **The most beautiful language in the register is search-dead.** *Golden hour* belongs to a
  Kacey Musgraves record. *Indian summer* belongs to a TV series, a golf course and a juice.
  *Altweibersommer* and *mono no aware* have no English search behaviour at all
- **The plain functional language carries all the search.** *end of summer 2026*, *late summer
  outfits women*, *fall jackets 2026*, and the fit modifiers
- **`[inference]` These are not in conflict — they are different layers.** The campaign is where
  the register lives and it is **never a search target**. The collections are where the plain
  nouns and modifiers live and they are the only rankable asset. Trying to make one string do
  both jobs is what produces slop
- **`[inference]` The strongest crossing point:** the register's central behaviour — *last-ness,
  the squeeze, permission, preserving* — shows up **directly** in the search data as the
  permission queries (*when can you start wearing white again*), the how-to-wear queries, the
  *end of summer decluttering* completion, and the +500% on collecting fragrance. **The feeling
  and the search behaviour are the same behaviour.** That is the finding of this survey
- **`[hunch]` The year-tag gap is an opening.** *late summer outfits 2025* still outranks any
  2026 variant. Nobody has accumulated the 2026 versions of these seasonal queries yet

## 4 · Candidate unifying ideas — campaign altitude

Six. Each is an ethos the collections would live inside, not a territory. **No names.**

**1 · The sweetness of the ending (mono no aware)**
Beauty *because* it is finishing. Elegiac, adult, unhurried.
→ Most emotionally sophisticated. Easiest to make mawkish. No search surface, by design.

**2 · The old woman's summer**
Multiple cultures independently named this fortnight after a woman past her youth having a
brief, undeniable spell. Beauty arriving late and knowing it is late.
→ Folklore has already built the entire world. Completely unclaimed in fashion. Casting and tone
risk is real and must be handled deliberately, not avoided.

**3 · The long light**
The year's best light, low and gold. A photographic ethos more than a verbal one.
→ Visually strongest. `[autocomplete]` confirms the words are unusable — which is fine at
campaign altitude but means it can never become a collection name.

**4 · Preserve it**
The keeping instinct — canning, archiving, holding the thing before it goes.
→ **The only candidate with a structural argument underneath it: saving is literally what our
users do on the platform. The save button IS the preserve instinct.** The season's core gesture
and the product's core gesture are the same gesture. Corroborated at `[vendor]` +500% on
collecting fragrance and `[autocomplete]` *end of summer decluttering*.

**5 · The turn**
The moment of change itself, held still. Two seasons in one frame.
→ Named by a live calendar as of Aug 7. Risk: "transitional dressing" is what every retailer
runs, and this can collapse into it.

**6 · September is the new year**
The reset, the sharpening, the becoming.
→ Most commercially proven, by far the most crowded, and adjacent to back-to-school, which the
register explicitly rules out.

### Read

`[inference]` **2 and 4 are the two that could only be ours.** 4 has a structural argument — the
platform's core gesture and the season's core instinct are identical. 2 has a fully built world
that nobody has claimed. 1 is the most beautiful and the most dangerous. 6 is what everyone else
will run.
