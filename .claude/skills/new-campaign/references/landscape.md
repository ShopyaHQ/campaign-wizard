# Stage 1 · Landscape — what is actually happening

The first stage of every run, without exception. Its job is **not** to produce a campaign.
Its job is to lay out the world our users are in right now, from several angles, so the human
can react to a spread rather than to a single idea I got attached to.

---

## The Stage 1 deliverable — locked shape, every run

One report, six sections, in this order. Written to `research/<yyyy-mm>_stage1_landscape.md`
(never to `campaigns/` — no campaign_id exists yet).

1. **The moment — campaign altitude.** The cultural register: what the calendar says versus the
   body · what other cultures NAME this moment · light · colour · sound · smell · taste · what
   people actually do · where the register already lives in art · **and what the moment is NOT**
2. **The evidence — collection altitude.** Autocomplete, runway, editorial, vendor data. Every
   item labelled. Contaminated terms called out. Unavailable data named as unavailable
3. **The crossing.** Where the register and the data meet, and where they do not. This is the
   analytical section and the reason the two altitudes are surveyed together
4. **Candidate unifying ideas.** Six, campaign altitude, **no names**, each with its risk stated
   and an honest read on which could only be ours
5. **Banked for Stage 4.** The collection and rail territory found along the way, explicitly
   marked as not campaign material
6. **Open questions**, including first-party data, and what to research next

**The name is the least important output of this stage — it is not an output of this stage at
all.** The context and the summary are the deliverable.

---

## What this stage produces

**A spread of candidate angles — six to eight.** Flat, unranked, unnamed. Each one is a
territory with a tension in it and evidence underneath it. The human kills some, merges some,
asks for more in a direction, and eventually points at one. That may take several rounds.

**Do not push toward a pick.** Presenting five weak angles and one obvious winner is steering,
not surveying. If one angle is genuinely stronger, say so plainly as an observation — but bring
the other seven anyway, and bring them at full strength.

## The altitude problem — read this before you research anything

**A campaign is not a trend. It is what brings the trends together.**

Three altitudes, and confusing them is the failure this section exists to stop:

| Altitude | What it is | Where it comes from |
|---|---|---|
| **Campaign** | the unifying feeling, the ethos, what the moment MEANS | art, film, literature, music, folk calendar, language, food, light, sound, smell |
| **Collection** | a territory inside it | trend reports, runway, autocomplete, editorial |
| **Rail** | a hook onto one territory | keyword strategy |

A trend report will never hand you a campaign. Runway roundups, Pinterest Predicts, autocomplete
and "what to buy this month" produce **collection and rail material** — real, valuable, and to be
banked in `research/` for Stage 4. But a list of things that are happening is not a point of view
about the moment they are all happening in.

**So Stage 1 sweeps BOTH altitudes, and labels which is which.**

### Where campaign material actually comes from

Not from retail sources. From the culture around the moment:

- **Art and film** — who has painted or shot this time of year, and what they saw in it
- **Literature and poetry** — the canonical texts for the moment
- **Language** — what other cultures NAME this moment. A culture that has a word for something
  has already done the emotional analysis for you. This is the single richest vein
- **Folk calendar and ritual** — what people traditionally do now, and why
- **The senses** — light, colour, sound, smell, taste. Specific and physical, never "vibes"
- **Behaviour** — what people actually do at this moment, and what that reveals they want

### The sensory register is mandatory

Before proposing any unifying idea, lay out the moment's **light, colour, sound, smell, taste**
in concrete, physical detail. Not adjectives — actual objects and phenomena. A campaign built
without this is built on abstraction, and abstraction is what produces slop.

State plainly what the moment is **NOT**, too. Every seasonal moment has an obvious commercial
reflex sitting next to it that everyone else runs. Name it so it can be avoided.

## What an angle is, and what it is not

**An angle IS:** a condition in the world — a season, a weather fact, a cultural moment, an
aesthetic movement, a shift in how people are behaving, an object having a moment, a backlash
against something — plus the tension inside it, plus what the data says about it.

**An angle IS NOT:** a category, a keyword, a name, a tagline, a product list, or a campaign.
If it can be typed into a search box it is a keyword, not an angle.

### Sweep these angle types so the spread is genuinely diverse

Do not return eight versions of the same thought. Cover:

- **Seasonal / weather** — what is literally true outside right now, and next month
- **Cultural moment** — events, releases, sport, awards, anniversaries, a place having a year
- **Aesthetic movement** — a look with a name and momentum, where it came from, who is doing it
- **Behavioural shift** — how people are shopping, dressing, decorating, travelling differently
- **Object-led** — a single item having a moment, and what it says that it is that item
- **Counter-trend / backlash** — what people are visibly tired of. Often the strongest angle,
  because it is the one nobody else is running
- **Revival** — something returning, and what is different about it the second time

## What is BANNED at this stage

Absolute, no exceptions. Mentioning any of these collapses concept work into merchandising,
which is the failure mode this entire process exists to prevent.

- **No names, no taglines, no campaign titles** — not even "working title", not even in brackets
- **No collections, no rails, no counts of either**
- **No `sub_group`, no vertical tagging, no `sort_order`, no render caps, no floors or targets**
- **No CSV, no admin, no execution talk**
- **No reference to what is in the existing library.** Not as a constraint, not as reassurance,
  not as "we already have some of this". The world does not care what we happen to hold
- **No product examples.** An angle is proved by evidence about the world, not by things we own

If a structural question genuinely blocks the survey, write it down as an open question. Do not
answer it here.

## Evidence — what is real and what is not

Every angle carries its evidence, and the evidence is labelled by type. An angle with no
evidence is a hunch; say so and let it stand as a hunch rather than dressing it as a finding.

**Real and available:**
- Google public autocomplete (`suggestqueries.google.com/complete/search?client=firefox&q=`) —
  genuine ranked ordering of what people type
- Editorial coverage via search — shows what is being written about, how saturated it is, and
  where the gaps are
- Retailer and platform category naming — a proxy for what is converting
- Dated cultural facts — release dates, event calendars, weather records

**Never fabricate:** search volume, keyword difficulty, rising-vs-flat trend direction, social
engagement numbers, sell-through. If it would need a paid tool, write "unavailable" — never a
plausible-looking estimate.

**Always ask:** does the client have first-party data — site search logs, Search Console,
on-platform save and click behaviour? What our own users do beats every external proxy, and it
is the one source that makes an angle ours rather than generic.

## Format

Per angle, short and scannable:

- **The condition** — one or two plain sentences on what is happening
- **The tension** — why it is interesting rather than merely true
- **Evidence** — bulleted, each item labelled `[autocomplete]` `[editorial]` `[fact]`
  `[inference]` `[hunch]`
- **Why it could be ours** — the discovery-platform read on it, or honestly: "generic so far"
- **What is thin** — what you could not verify

Then, separately from the angles: open questions, and what you would want to research next.

→ **GATE: the human reacts to the spread.** Kill, merge, redirect, or pick. Expect more than one
round. Nothing moves to the brief until the human names the angle.
